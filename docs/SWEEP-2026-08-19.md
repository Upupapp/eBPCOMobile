## S-01 · The product name — RULED AND CLOSED, 19 Aug 2026

**Owner's ruling: it is the Electronic Building Permit and Certificate of
Occupancy.** My reading was backwards — I concluded the app was right and the
spec wrong, because the app agreed with the surrounding documents. It agreed
with them because they were all wrong together. Weight of sources is not
evidence of correctness when they share an origin.

Corrected in the app: `appFullName`, `appTagline`, `splashSubtitle`, the
privacy policy, the terms, and the help screen. Three of those also named the
wrong *office* — "Business Permit and Licensing Office" — including the one a
data subject is directed to for RA 10173 requests; all now the Office of the
Building Official. Guarded by `test/shared/app_logo_test.dart`, which also
checks the longer name still fits a 360dp login screen at 200% text scale.

Two occurrences are outside this repo and left alone under the mobile-only
rule: the monorepo's `docs/README.md` and the web admin's `auth-layout.html`
tagline. **M-37.**

Original finding, kept for the record:

### The finding as first written — HIGH

`docs/eBPCO-Mobile-Master-Command.pdf` calls the system the **Electronic
Building Permit and Certificate of Occupancy**. Every other source in the
project says **Electronic Business Permit and Clearance Office**:

- `docs/README.md` in the monorepo, twice
- the web admin's own `auth-layout.html` tagline
- `lib/core/constants/app_strings.dart` — `appTagline`
- `lib/features/profile/presentation/terms_conditions_screen.dart`
- the mobile `README.md`

The app code is right and the spec document is wrong. It was written without
checking the project's own docs.

There is a real tension underneath it, which is probably why it slipped: the
*scope* is sixteen construction permits under PD 1096 plus a Certificate of
Occupancy, which is building-permit work, not business-permit work. So the
project's name and its contents genuinely disagree. That is an owner's call —
but it was resolved silently, in one direction, across a 24-page specification
presented as authoritative. **See M-27.**

Fix: correct the PDF and its HTML source, or get a ruling first. Do not
"correct" the app to match the PDF.

## S-02 · Six locally-derivable notifications are declared but never fire — HIGH

§9.3 requires every one of N-01…N-24 to be produced. Twelve have no producer.
Six of those the app can derive entirely on its own, and all the logic already
exists:

| Type | Derivation that already exists |
|---|---|
| `pledgeApproaching` | `ServicePledge.isDueSoon` |
| `pledgeLapsed` | `ServicePledge.hasLapsed` |
| `permitCommencementWarning` | `ApplicationModel.commenceByDate` |
| `professionalCredentialExpiring` | `ProfessionalModel.prcNeedsAttention` |
| `draftIdle` | draft state in the wizard providers |
| `occupancyNowPossible` | a released building permit |

Only `permitCommencementWarning` reaches the applicant at all, via the Home
action stack. A lapsed service pledge, an expiring PRC, an idle draft, and
"you may now file for occupancy" reach them **nowhere** — the conditions are
computed and then dropped.

Fix: a single evaluator that walks applications, professionals, and drafts on
load and records the matching events. The catalog and the derivations both
exist; nothing connects them.

## S-03 · Five absent notifications are correctly absent — record, do not "fix"

`fsecCleared`, `paymentVerified`, `permitGenerated`, `released`, and

correctly and will fire when a backend sends them. Manufacturing them

## S-04 · The iOS floor rose from 13.0 to 15.0 — CLOSED, and I had the cause wrong

I wrote that the plugin set forced it and called it an audience decision that
happened by accident. Both halves were wrong, and reverting the file and
rebuilding is what showed it:

```
Updating minimum iOS deployment target to 15.0.
Upgrading project.pbxproj
```

**Flutter does this itself.** `ios_deployment_target_migration.dart` in
Flutter 3.47 rewrites any target from 8.0 through 14.0 up to 15.0 on every
build. No plugin is involved: every podspec and `Package.swift` in this
project declares 12.0 or 13.0. Reverting is futile — the tool re-applies it.

So there is nothing to accept or reject. Holding 13.0 would mean downgrading
the Flutter SDK, which is not a trade anyone would make for this.

It also costs no devices. iOS 15 runs on every iPhone that ran iOS 13 —
6s and later — so the affected group is users who have declined a free OS
update on a supported handset, not users whose hardware is excluded.

Committed rather than left dirty. **M-38 closed.**
